Corollary 3.3.2

§3.3 The exterior angle theorem
Corollary 3.3.2

The sum of the measures of any two angles of a triangle is less than 180∘180\grado180∘. Consequently, every triangle has at most one right or obtuse angle, and at least two acute angles.

Proof

If ∠C′\ang C'∠C′ is the exterior angle at CCC, m∠B<m∠C′=180∘−m∠C\med\ang B<\med\ang C'=180\grado-\med\ang Cm∠B<m∠C′=180∘−m∠C (T4), that is, m∠B+m∠C<180∘\med\ang B+\med\ang C<180\gradom∠B+m∠C<180∘. If there were two angles of measure ≥90∘\geq90\grado≥90∘, their sum would be ≥180∘\geq180\grado≥180∘.

Definition 3.4.1 · Transversal and the eight angles

§3.4 Parallel lines
Definition 3.4.1 · Transversal and the eight angles

A transversal to two coplanar lines ℓ\ellℓ and mmm is a line that meets them at two distinct points. Eight angles are formed, which get these names:

  1. alternate interior: they lie between ℓ\ellℓ and mmm, on opposite sides of the transversal (∠3,∠5\ang 3,\ang 5∠3,∠5 and ∠4,∠6\ang 4,\ang 6∠4,∠6);

  2. alternate exterior: outside the strip, on opposite sides (∠1,∠7\ang 1,\ang 7∠1,∠7 and ∠2,∠8\ang 2,\ang 8∠2,∠8);

  3. corresponding: one interior and one exterior on the same side of the transversal (∠1,∠5\ang 1,\ang 5∠1,∠5; ∠2,∠6\ang 2,\ang 6∠2,∠6; ∠3,∠7\ang 3,\ang 7∠3,∠7; ∠4,∠8\ang 4,\ang 8∠4,∠8);

  4. same-side (or consecutive) interior: between ℓ\ellℓ and mmm, on the same side (∠4,∠5\ang 4,\ang 5∠4,∠5 and ∠3,∠6\ang 3,\ang 6∠3,∠6).

Theorem 3.4.2 · Alternate interior angles criterion

§3.4 Parallel lines
Theorem 3.4.2 · Alternate interior angles criterion

If two lines cut by a transversal form a pair of congruent alternate interior angles, then they are parallel.

Proof

Let ℓ\ellℓ and mmm be cut by ttt at PPP and QQQ, with a pair of congruent alternate interior angles.

Suppose, for a contradiction, that ℓ\ellℓ and mmm meet at a point RRR.

Then PPP, QQQ and RRR form a triangle, and one of the two alternate interior angles is an exterior angle of △PQR\tri PQR△PQR while the other is a remote interior angle.

By the exterior angle theorem, the first is greater than the second, which contradicts that they are congruent.

∎

Corollary 3.4.3

§3.4 Parallel lines
Corollary 3.4.3

Two lines cut by a transversal are parallel if any of the following conditions holds: a pair of corresponding angles is congruent; a pair of alternate exterior angles is congruent; a pair of same-side interior angles is supplementary. In particular, two lines perpendicular to the same line are parallel.

Proof

Each condition implies, using vertical angles or linear pairs, that a pair of alternate interior angles is congruent. For example, if the corresponding angles ∠1\ang1∠1 and ∠5\ang5∠5 are congruent, since ∠1≅∠3\ang1\cong\ang3∠1≅∠3 (vertical angles), we have ∠3≅∠5\ang3\cong\ang5∠3≅∠5, which are alternate interior angles.

∎

Parallel lines

§3.4 Parallel lines

This corollary gives, with no new postulate, the existence of parallels: given a line ℓ\ellℓ and a point P∉ℓP\notin\ellP∈/ℓ, we draw the perpendicular ttt from PPP to ℓ\ellℓ (Theorem 3.3.3) and then the perpendicular mmm to ttt through PPP (Theorem 2.4.3). The lines ℓ\ellℓ and mmm are perpendicular to ttt, hence parallel. What cannot be proved with the postulates we have is the uniqueness of that parallel.

Theorem 3.4.5 · Converse of the alternate interior angles criterion

Theorem 3.4.5 · Converse of the alternate interior angles criterion

If two parallel lines are cut by a transversal, the alternate interior angles are congruent. Consequently, corresponding angles and alternate exterior angles are congruent and same-side interior angles are supplementary.

Proof

Let ℓ∥m\ell\paralelo mℓ∥m be cut by ttt at P∈ℓP\in\ellP∈ℓ and Q∈mQ\in mQ∈m. By postulate T2 there is a line ℓ′\ell'ℓ′ through PPP forming with ttt an alternate interior angle congruent to the one formed by mmm. By Theorem 3.4.2, ℓ′∥m\ell'\paralelo mℓ′∥m. By Playfair’s postulate, ℓ′=ℓ\ell'=\ellℓ′=ℓ. Hence the alternate interior angles formed by ℓ\ellℓ are congruent. The other statements follow as in Corollary 3.4.3.

∎

Corollary 3.4.6

§3.4 Parallel lines
Corollary 3.4.6

  1. If a line is perpendicular to one of two parallels, it is perpendicular to the other.

  2. Two lines parallel to a third are parallel to each other (transitivity).

Proof

(i) If ℓ∥m\ell\paralelo mℓ∥m and t⊥ℓt\perp\ellt⊥ℓ, the line ttt meets mmm (otherwise, through the point of t∩ℓt\cap\ellt∩ℓ there would pass two parallels to mmm). The alternate interior angles are congruent, so ttt forms a right angle with mmm. (ii) If ℓ∥n\ell\paralelo nℓ∥n, m∥nm\paralelo nm∥n and ℓ\ellℓ, mmm met at a point PPP, through PPP there would pass two parallels to nnn. Hence ℓ∥m\ell\paralelo mℓ∥m.

∎

Corollary 3.5.2

§3.5 The angles of a triangle
Corollary 3.5.2

  1. (Exterior angle.) The measure of an exterior angle of a triangle equals the sum of the measures of the two remote interior angles.

  2. The acute angles of a right triangle are complementary.

  3. Each angle of an equilateral triangle measures 60∘60\grado60∘.

  4. If two angles of a triangle are congruent to two angles of another, the third angles are congruent as well.

Proof

(i) The exterior angle at CCC measures 180∘−m∠C=m∠A+m∠B180\grado-\med\ang C=\med\ang A+\med\ang B180∘−m∠C=m∠A+m∠B. The rest are immediate computations.

∎

Theorem 3.6.1 · The larger side is opposite the larger angle

§3.6 Inequalities in a triangle
Theorem 3.6.1 · The larger side is opposite the larger angle

If two sides of a triangle are not congruent, the angles opposite them are not congruent either, and the larger angle is opposite the larger side: if AB>ACAB>ACAB>AC, then m∠C>m∠B\med\ang C>\med\ang Bm∠C>m∠B.

Proof

Since AC<ABAC<ABAC<AB, there is DDD on AB‾\seg{AB}AB, between AAA and BBB, with AD=ACAD=ACAD=AC. The triangle △ADC\tri ADC△ADC is isosceles, so ∠ADC≅∠ACD\ang ADC\cong\ang ACD∠ADC≅∠ACD. Since DDD is in the interior of ∠ACB\ang ACB∠ACB, m∠C>m∠ACD=m∠ADC\med\ang C>\med\ang ACD=\med\ang ADCm∠C>m∠ACD=m∠ADC. And ∠ADC\ang ADC∠ADC is an exterior angle of the triangle △DBC\tri DBC△DBC, so m∠ADC>m∠B\med\ang ADC>\med\ang Bm∠ADC>m∠B. Putting it together, m∠C>m∠B\med\ang C>\med\ang Bm∠C>m∠B.

∎

Corollary 3.6.2 · The larger angle is opposite the larger side

§3.6 Inequalities in a triangle
Corollary 3.6.2 · The larger angle is opposite the larger side

If m∠C>m∠B\med\ang C>\med\ang Bm∠C>m∠B, then AB>ACAB>ACAB>AC. In particular, in a right triangle the hypotenuse is the longest side.

Proof

By trichotomy, AB=ACAB=ACAB=AC, AB<ACAB<ACAB<AC or AB>ACAB>ACAB>AC. If AB=ACAB=ACAB=AC, the triangle would be isosceles and ∠B≅∠C\ang B\cong\ang C∠B≅∠C. If AB<ACAB<ACAB<AC, the theorem would give m∠B>m∠C\med\ang B>\med\ang Cm∠B>m∠C. Both possibilities contradict the hypothesis; we are left with AB>ACAB>ACAB>AC.

∎

In every triangle, the length of each side is less than the sum of the lengths of the other two: AB+BC>ACAB+BC>ACAB+BC>AC.

Theorem 3.7.1 · Theorem of the oblique segments

Let P∉ℓP\notin\ellP∈/ℓ and let HHH be the foot of the perpendicular from PPP to ℓ\ellℓ. For every point Q∈ℓQ\in\ellQ∈ℓ other than HHH:

  1. PQ>PHPQ>PHPQ>PH (the perpendicular segment is the shortest);

  2. if Q,R∈ℓQ,R\in\ellQ,R∈ℓ, then PQ=PRPQ=PRPQ=PR if and only if HQ=HRHQ=HRHQ=HR, and PQ>PRPQ>PRPQ>PR if and only if HQ>HRHQ>HRHQ>HR (of two oblique segments, the longer is the one whose foot is farther away).

Proof

(i) In the triangle △PHQ\tri PHQ△PHQ the angle at HHH is right, so it is the largest and PQ‾\seg{PQ}PQ​, the hypotenuse, is the longest side (Corollary 3.6.2).

(ii) If HQ=HRHQ=HRHQ=HR, the right triangles △PHQ\tri PHQ△PHQ and △PHR\tri PHR△PHR are congruent by SAS and PQ=PRPQ=PRPQ=PR; the converse follows from the hypotenuse-leg criterion.

For the inequality, if HQ>HRHQ>HRHQ>HR with QQQ and RRR on the same side of HHH, then H−R−QH{-}R{-}QH−R−Q; the angle ∠PRQ\ang PRQ∠PRQ is exterior to the triangle △PHR\tri PHR△PHR and therefore obtuse, so in △PRQ\tri PRQ△PRQ the longest side is PQ‾\seg{PQ}PQ​.

Theorem 3.7.3 · Distance between parallels

If ℓ∥m\ell\paralelo mℓ∥m, all points of ℓ\ellℓ are at the same distance from mmm. That number is the distance between the parallels.

Proof

Let P,Q∈ℓP,Q\in\ellP,Q∈ℓ and H,KH,KH,K be the feet of their perpendiculars on mmm. By Corollary 3.4.6, PH↔\recta{PH}PH and QK↔\recta{QK}QK​ are also perpendicular to ℓ\ellℓ. In the triangles △PHK\tri PHK△PHK and △KQP\tri KQP△KQP: PK‾\seg{PK}PK is common; ∠HKP≅∠QPK\ang HKP\cong\ang QPK∠HKP≅∠QPK (alternate interior angles between the parallels ℓ\ellℓ and mmm with transversal PK↔\recta{PK}PK) and ∠PHK≅∠KQP\ang PHK\cong\ang KQP∠PHK≅∠KQP (both right). By SAA, △PHK≅△KQP\tri PHK\cong\tri KQP△PHK≅△KQP, and PH=KQPH=KQPH=KQ.

§3.8 Angle bisector and perpendicular bisector as loci

A locus is the set of all points satisfying a property. Proving that a figure is a certain locus requires proving two inclusions: that every point of the figure satisfies the property, and that every point satisfying the property lies on the figure.

Theorem 3.8.1 · Characterization of the perpendicular bisector

In a plane, the perpendicular bisector of a segment AB‾\seg{AB}AB is the set of points equidistant from AAA and BBB: P∈mediatriz de AB‾ ⟺ PA=PB.P\in\text{mediatriz de }\seg{AB}\ \sii\ PA=PB.P∈mediatriz de AB ⟺ PA=PB.

Proof

Let MMM be the midpoint of AB‾\seg{AB}AB and mmm the perpendicular bisector.

(⇒\Rightarrow⇒) Let P∈mP\in mP∈m.

If P=MP=MP=M, it is clear.

Otherwise, the triangles △PMA\tri PMA△PMA and △PMB\tri PMB△PMB have PM‾\seg{PM}PM in common and right angles at MMM and MA‾≅MB‾\seg{MA}\cong\seg{MB}MA≅MB; by SAS they are congruent and PA=PBPA=PBPA=PB.

Theorem 3.8.2 · Characterization of the angle bisector

Theorem 3.8.2 · Characterization of the angle bisector

A point PPP in the interior of an angle ∠ABC\ang ABC∠ABC lies on the bisector if and only if it is equidistant from the sides of the angle: d(P,BA↔)=d(P,BC↔)d(P,\recta{BA})=d(P,\recta{BC})d(P,BA)=d(P,BC).

Proof

Let EEE and FFF be the feet of the perpendiculars from PPP to BA↔\recta{BA}BA and BC↔\recta{BC}BC. (⇒\Rightarrow⇒) If ∠PBE≅∠PBF\ang PBE\cong\ang PBF∠PBE≅∠PBF, the right triangles △PBE\tri PBE△PBE and △PBF\tri PBF△PBF have the common hypotenuse BP‾\seg{BP}BP and are congruent by SAA; hence PE=PFPE=PFPE=PF. (⇐\Leftarrow⇐) If PE=PFPE=PFPE=PF, the same triangles are congruent by hypotenuse-leg, and ∠PBE≅∠PBF\ang PBE\cong\ang PBF∠PBE≅∠PBF.

∎
